## What

Ten `best-motherboard-for-*.html` pages ship a **"price range" table** stating
what a reader should expect to pay per tier, and `reviews.html` ships a **price
filter** with hardcoded bands. None of them carries a date, a source, or a
retailer. Verified against the tree on 2026-08-21:

`best-motherboard-for-core-ultra-9-285k.html:189-192`

    Flagship   Z890   $500-700
    High-End   Z890   $350-500
    Mid-Range  Z890   $200-350
    Value      B860   $150-250

`best-motherboard-for-ryzen-9-9950x.html:189-191`

    Flagship   X870E  $450-550
    High-End   X870E  $400-450
    Mid-Range  X870   $250-350

`best-motherboard-for-ryzen-7-7800x3d.html:189-191`

    Premium    X670E  $350-450
    Sweet Spot B650   $180-250
    Budget     B650   $130-180

Same shape on `best-motherboard-for-core-ultra-5-245k.html`,
`best-motherboard-for-i7-14700k.html`, `best-motherboard-for-i9-9900k.html`,
`best-motherboard-for-ryzen-5-3600.html`, `best-motherboard-for-ryzen-5-5600x.html`,
`best-motherboard-for-ryzen-7-3700x.html`, `best-motherboard-for-ryzen-7-9700x.html`.

Plus loose price claims in prose and tip lists, e.g.:

- `best-motherboard-for-core-ultra-9-285k.html:153` — "If you want Z890 overclocking without spending $500+..."
- `best-motherboard-for-i5-12400f.html:176` — "DDR4 boards save $50+ vs DDR5 (board + RAM)"
- `best-motherboard-for-ryzen-5-5600x.html:164` — "WiFi models add $20-30; skip if you have ethernet"

And the filter buckets in `reviews.html:166-169` — Under $150 / $150-$250 /
$250-$400 / Over $400.

**#24 explicitly declared these out of scope**, on the reasoning that they are
"structural buckets rather than live price claims." That call was reasonable when
it was made. This issue asks to revisit it, because the 2026 board market has
since moved far enough that the buckets are arguably making a factual claim that
is no longer true.

## Why

1. **CLAUDE.md rule 5 is about honest dates, and these have none.** A reader has
   no way to tell whether "$500-700 for a flagship Z890" was researched last week
   or at launch. Presented in a table with no date, it reads as current.
2. **The board market has moved hard in both directions during 2026, and we have
   reporting for both.** #24 already documents reporting from 2026-08-04 that
   ASUS, GIGABYTE and MSI plan Q3 2026 board price *increases* (driven by PCB
   costs, not a 50% retail rise — see #24 for the misreporting correction).
   Pulling the other way, Tom's Hardware is currently running a piece headlined
   "The motherboard market is so bad that flagship boards are selling at
   entry-level prices — save up to 57% of premium motherboard designs while RAM
   prices surge," attributing deep premium-tier discounting to collapsed build
   demand as RAM and SSD prices spike. Either way, a two-year-old undated band is
   not describing today.
3. **The buckets drive a live UI, not just prose.** `reviews.html` filters cards
   by price band. If the bands are stale the filter silently mis-sorts boards,
   which is a worse failure than stale prose because the reader can't see it.
4. **It is a prerequisite for the deal work.** #24 defines a dated deal-note
   pattern for review pages. Shipping a dated, sourced price note next to an
   undated, unsourced price band on a roundup page would look incoherent.

## Verification caveat — read before acting

The scout **could not verify a single current retail price**, and this issue
deliberately asserts none:

- The Tom's Hardware article above could not be read — the fetch returned only
  the site header and nav, no article body, so the **publication date and the
  named models are unconfirmed**. Only the headline is attested.
- One search summary attributed a specific figure to it (ASRock Z890 Taichi at
  $200, down from $460, ~56% off — a board we cover at
  `review-asrock-z890-taichi.html`). **Treat this as unverified.** A different
  summary of the same article described the discount as a Prime Day offer, which
  would make it stale by now. Do not put that number, or any number from a search
  summary, on a page.
- Amazon and other retailer listings returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches, as
  #24 already found. There is currently no automated route to a citable retail
  price for a specific board.

This is exactly why the fix below is mostly about **removing an implied claim**
rather than publishing better numbers.

## Why needs-plan

This is a design decision the issue does not settle, and it touches 11+ files —
both CLAUDE.md tripwires. There are at least three defensible answers and the
right one is a judgement call:

- **(a) Date-stamp them.** Add "Price ranges checked <date>" above each table and
  a staleness rule, reusing whatever #24 settles on. Cheapest, but commits us to
  re-checking 10 pages on a cadence, with no automated price source to do it with.
- **(b) De-claim them.** Reword the tables so they describe *tiers* rather than
  *current prices* — the useful content in that table is "flagship vs mid-range
  vs value and what you get", which survives without a dollar figure. Loses some
  reader utility, gains permanence.
- **(c) Remove them.** Simplest, most defensible, biggest content loss.

`reviews.html`'s filter is a separate sub-decision: the bands there are
functional UI, so (b) and (c) do not apply cleanly to it.

- [ ] Whatever is chosen, no dollar figure may remain on a page implying it is
      current without a visible date checked.
- [ ] No price may be published unless verified against the retailer listing at
      the time of writing — same sourcing rule #24 sets. Deal-aggregator and
      "best deals" round-ups are not acceptable sources.
- [ ] Decide separately what to do about `reviews.html:166-169`. If the filter
      bands stay as-is, say so explicitly and explain why in the PR rather than
      leaving it unaddressed.
- [ ] Also sweep the loose in-prose figures listed above; do not fix the tables
      and leave "$500+" sitting in a paragraph two screens up.
- [ ] Coordinate with #24 so roundup pages and review pages use one convention,
      not two.
- [ ] Per CLAUDE.md rule 5, bump "Updated" dates only on pages whose content
      actually changed. See #84.
- [ ] `npm run validate` green.
- [ ] Touches more than 15 files if the prose sweep is included — flag
      `needs-human` before merge per the CLAUDE.md tripwire.
