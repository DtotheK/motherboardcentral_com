Overview

The ASUS TUF Gaming B850-Plus WiFi is a mainstream ATX board for Socket AM5, aimed at Ryzen 9000, 8000 and 7000 builds that want a full-size layout without paying for a flagship chipset. It pairs 14+2+1 power stages with three M.2 slots, four DIMM slots and Wi-Fi 7. This page is based on published specifications from ASUS; we have not handled this board.

Security advisory: check your BIOS

AMD published bulletin AMD-SB-7064 on 11 August 2026. It covers two high-severity flaws in AMD's firmware TPM: CVE-2026-6726, an information-leakage flaw that lets a forged TPM key be certified as genuine, and CVE-2026-6727, a timing side-channel. AMD scores them 8.5 and 8.3 on CVSS 4.0. Affected processors run from Ryzen 3000 to Ryzen 9000, plus Threadripper, Ryzen AI and Ryzen Embedded parts. Both attacks need local privileged access, so neither is remotely exploitable. The underlying flaw is in the Trusted Computing Group's TPM 2.0 reference code, which AMD ships as fTPM.

The fix arrives as a motherboard BIOS update. Windows Update will not install it. Check ASUS's security advisories and this board's own support page, install the latest BIOS, then look for an fTPM or TPM security fix in the release notes. Coverage varies by board, and we have not verified that a fixed BIOS exists for this one.

Before you flash: if this machine uses BitLocker, save your recovery key first. A firmware update can trigger BitLocker recovery, and Windows will then ask for a 48-digit key before it unlocks the drive. Our BIOS update guide covers the safe order.

Design & Build Quality

This is a full 12 x 9.6 inch ATX board, so it fits any standard mid-tower without the header crowding that mATX layouts force. ASUS fits VRM heatsinks and M.2 heatsinks as standard, and the primary PCIe slot uses a lever-released latch that makes removing a long graphics card easier.

The graphics slot on this board is PCIe 5.0 x16 with a Ryzen 9000 or 7000 Series processor. Fit a Ryzen 8000 Series chip and the same slot drops to PCIe 4.0 running in x8/x4 mode, because the lane budget comes from the CPU rather than the chipset. The second full-length slot is fed by the chipset and runs at x4 regardless of CPU.

Storage is three M.2 slots. Only the first is PCIe 5.0 x4, and only with a Ryzen 9000 or 7000 Series processor; the remaining two are PCIe 4.0 x4. Four SATA 6Gb/s ports cover older drives.

VRM & Power Delivery

ASUS specifies 14+2+1 power stages rated at 80A each. That is generous for the tier and leaves headroom for the top Ryzen 9000 parts, including the X3D models that mainstream buyers most often pair with a board at this level.

Connectivity & I/O

Wireless is Wi-Fi 7 with Bluetooth 5.4, and wired networking is a Realtek 2.5 Gigabit Ethernet controller. The back panel carries 10 USB ports: one 20Gbps Type-C, three 10Gbps Type-A, four 5Gbps Type-A and two USB 2.0. Audio is handled by the Realtek ALC1220P codec.

One detail worth checking against your case before you buy: USB4 on this board is provided as an internal header for a front-panel connector, not as a port you can plug into on the back panel. If you need 40Gbps Type-C without adding hardware, look at a board that exposes it directly.

Pros & Cons

Pros

  • 14+2+1 power stages at 80A, generous for a mainstream board
  • Three M.2 slots and four SATA ports for a full storage loadout
  • Wi-Fi 7 with Bluetooth 5.4
  • Realtek 2.5 Gigabit Ethernet
  • Four DIMM slots supporting up to 256GB of DDR5

Cons

  • USB4 is an internal header only — there is no 40Gbps Type-C on the back panel
  • Two of the three M.2 slots are PCIe 4.0 x4, not PCIe 5.0
  • The second full-length slot runs at x4 from the chipset, not x8 or x16
  • ALC1220P is a step below the ALC4080 codec used on the ROG Strix B850 boards

Who Should Buy This Board?

Builders putting together a Ryzen 9000 gaming or workstation system who want a full ATX layout, strong power delivery and current wireless, and who do not need a 40Gbps Type-C port on the back panel or more than three M.2 slots.

Build Tips & Recommendations

  • AM5 uses the same cooler mounting pattern as AM4, so most AM4 coolers fit without a new bracket. Check the manufacturer's compatibility list first.
  • Enable EXPO in BIOS to run your DDR5 kit at its rated speed. Two DIMMs generally clock higher than four on AM5.
  • Install your OS drive in the first M.2 slot to get PCIe 5.0 x4 bandwidth, which needs a Ryzen 9000 or 7000 Series CPU. The other two slots are PCIe 4.0 x4 and are better suited to game libraries.
  • If you want a 40Gbps Type-C port at the front of your case, make sure the case has a compatible front-panel module to connect to the internal USB4 header.
  • Update to the latest BIOS before installing components. This board supports BIOS FlashBack, so you can flash from a USB drive without a CPU installed.
