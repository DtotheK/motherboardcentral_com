## DECISIONS

### D1. Publish now or wait? → **Publish now, as a status tracker — not as a spec page.**

We ship a page whose subject is *the state of the information*, not the hardware. It prints, in our own
voice, only what Intel has said publicly. It names the five reported chipset tiers. **It prints no
leaked number of any kind** — no lane counts, no DMI figures, no SATA/USB counts, no Thunderbolt
version.

Reasoning: the SEO argument for shipping early is real and the trust argument against publishing leaks
is also real, but they are not actually in tension — search demand right now is for "what is LGA 1954 /
Intel 900 series", and an honest answer to that question ("here is what is confirmed, here is what is
one leaker's spreadsheet, here is why we won't print it") is a *better* page than a rumour table, and
one we never have to retract. This is the same call the site already made and shipped on
`preview-gigabyte-b550-x-series.html`: *"where GIGABYTE has not published a number, we do not print
one."* Holding entirely forfeits the ranking window for no additional safety, because the safety comes
from the labelling policy, not from the delay.

### D2. Page shape? → **One hub page. No per-chipset children. W980/Q970 named only.**

Reasoning: five child pages built on a single leaker's February 2026 post would be five thin pages we
have to rewrite from scratch at launch, and thin near-duplicate pages are an active ranking liability.
Children get created when Intel publishes per-chipset spec pages and we have real material — that is a
follow-up issue, not this one. W980 (workstation) and Q970 (commercial vPro) are outside our audience —
we cover zero W/Q boards today — so they appear in the tier list for completeness and get one clause
each, no section.

### D3. URL and nav placement? → **`guide-intel-900-series.html`. No main-nav change.**

Reasoning on the slug: the existing pre-release precedent uses a `preview-` prefix, and I am
deliberately **not** following it here. That prefix is right for a page about one product range that
will later be superseded by review pages; it is wrong for a permanent platform hub, because at launch
we would want to rename it, and this site has no redirect layer configured (Vercel static, no
`vercel.json` in the repo) while CLAUDE.md forbids deleting pages. Picking the post-launch-correct URL
now avoids that trap entirely. The pre-release status lives in the `<title>`, the hero, and the status
box — all of which are cheap to update — not in the URL, which is not.

On nav: **do not touch the navbar.** It is four items across every page in the repo and shared-template
edits are an explicit CLAUDE.md tripwire; a pre-release page does not earn top-level nav. Discovery
comes from the guides grid, the sockets guide, and the sitemap — exactly how `preview-gigabyte-b550-x-series.html`
is surfaced today.

### D4. Confidence labelling convention? → **Three tiers, plus a one-source rule. Reuse existing CSS.**

This is the reusable convention for all future pre-launch content:

| Tier | Definition | What we may do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmed** | Stated by the vendor on its own materials — newsroom post, product/spec page, ARK entry, or an on-the-record executive statement in an earnings call | May be written in our own voice, may go in a spec table |
| **Reported** | Carried by named outlets that identify their source | Attributed inline every time — who said it, when — and **never** placed in a spec table |
| **Rumoured** | Anonymous, single-source, or unverifiable | May be named, never quantified. No numbers, ever |

**The one-source rule:** *N outlets repeating one leaker is one source, not N.* Independence is counted
at the origin, not at the byline. This rule is what disqualifies the entire 900-series spec table below
(see C2), and it is the most important half of the convention — it is exactly the failure mode that
would have bitten us here.

**Graduation:** a claim moves up a tier only when it appears on a vendor first-party page. When a claim
graduates, the "Updated" date changes; nothing else changes it.

Mechanically: use the existing `.info-box warning` status block above the first `<h2>` and `.info-box tip`
for asides, matching `preview-gigabyte-b550-x-series.html:86`. **Do not add a CSS class or touch
`css/style.css`** — shared-stylesheet edits are a tripwire and this convention does not need one.

### D5. Update/maintenance policy? → **Dated status line + 4-weekly re-verify, owned by market-watch.**

The status box carries "**Status as of 20 August 2026**" as its own sentence, separate from the
"Published … / Last updated …" line. Re-verification cadence: every 4 weeks until launch, and
immediately on any Intel announcement — filed as a recurring item against `jobs/market-watch.md`, which
already exists for exactly this. Per CLAUDE.md rule 5, a re-verification that finds nothing new updates
**nothing** — not the "Updated" date, not the "Status as of" date. Only a prose change moves either.

### D6. Downstream impact on Z890/B860/sockets/compare? → **Two additive edits now. Zero re-labelling.**

Reasoning: Nova Lake is not released. Calling Z890/B860 "previous generation" today would be false, and
it would devalue ~20 review pages that describe boards a reader can buy this afternoon. Generational
re-labelling is triggered by *retail availability*, not by our page going live — filed as a follow-up.

For now: one link in `guide-sockets.html`'s Related Guides, one sentence after its compatibility table,
one card in `guides.html`, one `sitemap.xml` entry. **LGA 1954 does not go in the socket compatibility
table** — that table is the page's factual spec table, and an unreleased socket does not belong in it.
(Note that our validator's spec-contradiction check only inspects prose *after* the first `</table>`,
so a bad table row would not even be caught — which is a reason for policy discipline here, not a
loophole to use.) `compare.html` is a comparison tool for boards we cover; there is no natural slot and
no board to compare — **leave it alone**.

## CAUTIONS

**C1 — The socket line is the one thing I could not pin to a first-party page, and it is in the
"confirmed" section.** `intel.com` and `newsroom.intel.com` have **no** Nova Lake or Core Ultra Series 4
material as of today — the newest desktop material there is Core Ultra Series 3 / 18A from CES 2026.
The LGA 1954 name is attributed to Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan on an earnings call by TechSpot
(`techspot.com/news/109998-...`), which is on-the-record and therefore Confirmed-tier under D4 — but one
of my searches asserted the opposite, that "neither LGA1954 nor the exact 900-series tiering… have been
officially confirmed by Intel to date." I could not resolve that disagreement against a primary
transcript. **Conservative instruction, and the worker must follow it:** re-check for an Intel
first-party page or an earnings-call transcript at implementation time. If neither is found, move the
socket name out of "What Intel has confirmed" and into "reported", attributed to the TechSpot
earnings-call report. The page is correct either way; only the section it sits in changes.

**C2 — The issue body's Thunderbolt claim does not survive verification, and nothing should be copied
from it.** The issue states *"Z990: … Thunderbolt 5 (up to 120 Gbps)"*. The primary leak reporting
(Club386's write-up of the @jaykihn0 post, and TweakTown's) says **USB4 / Thunderbolt 4** — no
Thunderbolt 5 anywhere. That is a direct contradiction inside our own issue tracker, and it is precisely
why D1 forbids printing leaked numbers. It also means **no future page may treat this issue body as a
source.** Whoever wrote it should know the TB5 line is unsupported.

**C3 — Every "source" in the issue is one source.** Tom's Hardware, Igor's Lab, WCCFTech, OC3D,
TweakTown, TechPowerUp, VideoCardz, Guru3D, HWCooling and the Wikipedia LGA 1954 stub all trace to a
single leaker (@jaykihn0) posting on 9 February 2026. Six links in the issue body reads like corroboration
and is not. This is the concrete case that motivates the one-source rule in D4.

**C4 — Launch timing is genuinely unsettled and I chose the conservative wording.** Intel says 2H/late
2026. Wikipedia's LGA 1954 stub says Q1 2027. Multiple outlets note leaks pointing at a January 2027
slip. The page should say "second half of 2026, per Intel" and explicitly note that retail availability
may land later — it must not print a specific retail date, which Task 1's regex enforces.

**C5 — Unverified: whether a Core Ultra Series 3 *desktop* part exists that we don't cover.** An Intel
newsroom headline from CES 2026 reads "Intel Core Ultra Series 3 Debut as First Built on Intel 18A." I
believe that is Panther Lake and mobile-only, and that Arrow Lake / Z890 is still Intel's current
desktop platform — but I did not confirm it, and it is out of scope for #29. If Series 3 desktop parts
do exist, that is a much more urgent content gap than Nova Lake and deserves its own issue. **Worth a
human glance.**

**C6 — I chose a slug that breaks with the existing `preview-` precedent.** Deliberate, reasoned in D3,
but it is the one decision here a human might reasonably reverse. Reversing it is cheap **only before
the page ships** — after that it needs a redirect this site cannot currently serve. If you disagree with
`guide-intel-900-series.html`, say so on this issue before the worker starts.
