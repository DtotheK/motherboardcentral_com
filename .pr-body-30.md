## Sourcing — two places this is narrower than the issue

**1. Only ASUS could be read directly.** MSI, GIGABYTE and ASRock all block automated fetching of their support pages (403, or JS-only pages that return empty). Per the issue's rule — "where only secondary sources exist, either verify against the vendor page or omit the claim" — all step-by-step LED procedures on the page are ASUS-attributed and taken from https://www.asus.com/support/faq/1042678/. The other three vendors get their feature named and their own support page linked, and nothing more:

- MSI EZ Debug LED → https://us.msi.com/faq/2504
- GIGABYTE status LEDs → https://www.gigabyte.com/Support/Consumer/FAQ/3151
- ASRock POST Status Checker / Dr. Debug → https://www.asrock.com/support/index.asp?cat=Debug

The cgdirector/technewstoday claim that MSI's LEDs light in sequence and that a brief flash of each is normal is **omitted** — it is secondary-only. The equivalent useful point (a *stuck* light marks the failed stage) is kept, sourced to MSI's own wording that the corresponding indicator stays constantly on.

**2. The XDA article does not support the DRAM-LED half of the AM5 caveat.** The issue cites https://www.xda-developers.com/this-am5-bios-change-halves-your-boot-time/ (25 Jan 2026) for "a long first boot with the DRAM LED lit is usually normal memory training". Reading it: it covers memory training with EXPO enabled and Memory Context Restore, but says nothing about LEDs. So the page splits the claim — the LED behaviour is attributed to ASUS's ROG article on AM5 memory training (Q-CODE 15 / yellow DRAM Q-LED, five minutes or more on a first boot or after a CMOS clear), and XDA is cited only for the EXPO/every-boot and MCR points it actually makes.

**Flagging for the reviewer:** the AM5 LED source is an ASUS-hosted ROG community article rather than an asus.com support FAQ. It is the closest first-party source found. Worth a look if you want that tightened.
