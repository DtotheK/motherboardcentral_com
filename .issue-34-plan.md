# DECISIONS

## 1. The affected scope IS published, at chipset-family granularity, in the CVE records — and it does not match the list in this issue

Verified today at NVD (published 16–17 December 2025, last modified 17 June 2026):

| Vendor | CVE | Affected scope, verbatim from the CVE record |
|---|---|---|
| ASUS | CVE-2025-11901 | "certain ASUS motherboards using Intel B460, B560, B660, **B760**, H410, H510, H610, H470, Z590, Z690, **Z790**, W480, W680 series chipsets" |
| GIGABYTE | CVE-2025-14302 | Intel 600 / 700 / 800 chipsets; AMD 600 / 800 / TRX50 chipsets |
| MSI | CVE-2025-14303 | Intel 600 / 700 chipsets |
| ASRock | CVE-2025-14304 | ASRock, ASRockRack, ASRockInd — Intel 500 / 600 / 700 / 800 chipsets |

Three corrections to the issue body, all of which change the page list:

- **ASUS is wider than reported, and narrower.** The issue lists ASUS as "Z490, Z590, Z690, Z790, W790". The CVE record does **not** name Z490 or W790, and **does** name B760 plus eight other families. We own no Z490 or W790 board, so the omissions cost nothing — but **B760 brings two ASUS pages into scope that this issue's list would have excluded.**
- **ASRock's scope is published after all.** The issue says ASRock is "status unknown, no vendor statement". That is true of the *remediation* status, but CVE-2025-14304 states the affected chipset scope plainly (Intel 500/600/700/800). ASRock owners are not left with nothing; they are left without a fix timeline. Different problem, different sentence.
- **All four CVEs carry `AV:P` in their CVSS vectors.** Physical access is not a press inference — it is in the CNA-assigned vector. This is the most important claim in our copy and it now rests on a primary source.

**Reasoning:** the issue's instruction was "no inference from chipset family alone." I am not inferring the family — the CNA *published* at the family level. What remains genuinely un-inferable is the model list *within* each family, because every record says "certain motherboard models," and the model lists live only on the vendor pages we cannot open. Decision 4 is built entirely around that gap.

## 2. Yes, this is still worth doing eight months on — and the reason is not urgency

The issue asks a human to decide whether to close this as stale. My answer is proceed, and the argument is not "it's a vulnerability."

A BIOS update alone does not fix this. Per CERT/CC, ASUS's remediation requires flashing **and then** setting IOMMU DMA Protection to "Enable with Full Protection" in BIOS Setup. That is a manual step a reader will never discover on their own, it does not decay with time, and it is invisible to anyone who updated their BIOS in January and assumed they were done. **A reader who patched eight months ago is the single most likely person to still be exposed.** Staleness is an argument against news; it is not an argument against a durable configuration instruction.

The counter-argument — physical access only, so low real-world risk — is real, and it is why decision 6 puts this on a hub page rather than a red box on 32 review pages.

## 3. Architecture: one hub page, plus a short pointer box per affected review page. Not 32 copies of the advisory

Same shape the planner chose for the AMD fTPM advisory on #22, and for the same reasons — retirement becomes a one-file edit, and 32 copies of a detailed advisory is 32 chances for the text to drift, which CLAUDE.md rule 1 makes a correctness bug rather than an aesthetic one.

There is an additional reason specific to this issue: **the four vendors' scopes differ from each other**, so a single byte-identical block genuinely cannot carry the truth. The nuance has to live in one place.

- **New page: `uefi-dma-security-advisory.html`.** Undated slug — it will be revised, not replaced. This deliberately parallels `amd-ftpm-security-advisory.html` from #22, establishing `<topic>-security-advisory.html` as the convention for this page type. There is no redirect layer in this repo, so the slug is permanent; pick it once, correctly.

## 4. The per-page box never says "this board is affected." It says "check your model"

Every CVE record says "**certain** motherboard models." The model lists are on the vendor pages, which are unreachable. So a page-level claim that a specific board is affected would be exactly the inference CLAUDE.md rule 1 and this issue both forbid.

The box therefore states the vendor's published chipset scope, attributed to the CVE ID, and sends the reader to that vendor's advisory to check their own model. That is honest, it is useful, and it cannot go stale.

AC 1 says the vendor advisory pages must be opened by hand and their model lists recorded in this issue **before any page is edited**. I cannot open them. This also means the reader-facing work is not blocked on a task only a human can do. The hub page is discoverable from the guides index and from the BIOS-update guide, which already has a bullet about security advisories waiting for exactly this link.

## 6. Markup: reuse the plain `<div class="info-box">`. `css/style.css` is not touched at all

`.info-box` (neutral) is the site's "read this" box; `.info-box.warning` (orange) is used 106× and is the Cons list on review pages. ## 7. Placement of the Phase 2 box: immediately before `<h2 id="specifications">`

Two reasons, one editorial and one mechanical:

- Mechanical: `scripts/validate.mjs:231` computes `afterTable = html.split(/<\/table>/i).slice(1)`, so the spec-contradiction checker only reads prose **after the first `</table>`**. A block placed above the spec table is structurally exempt. Belt and braces on top of decision 15.

## 8. CVSS scores: omit from all published copy

Every record carries **two** CNA-assigned scores on two scales — CVSS 4.0 **7.0 HIGH** and CVSS 3.1 **6.8 MEDIUM** — and CERT/CC's page quotes only the 6.8. Publishing "7.0 High" alone overstates it against the vendor's own 3.1 rating; publishing both is noise for a motherboard buyer.

Note this differs from #20/#22, where the scores were *unverifiable*. Here they are verified and merely unhelpful. **This is the cheapest decision in the plan to overrule** — if you want "rated 7.0 (High) under CVSS 4.0, 6.8 (Medium) under CVSS 3.1" in the copy, say so and it's a one-line change. I left it out because what a reader acts on is "physical access, plus a setting you have to turn on yourself," and a number crowds that out.

## 9. The "Enable with Full Protection" step is attributed to ASUS, not generalised to all four vendors

**This corrects the issue body.** The issue says "Per the vendor advisories, users must flash the patched firmware *and then* manually set IOMMU DMA Protection." CERT/CC attributes that instruction to **ASUS's statement specifically**. I checked the CNA advisories for the other three: TWCERT/CC's GIGABYTE, MSI and ASRock notices say only "Please update firmware to latest version" — no manual setting mentioned.

So the copy says: ASUS documents an additional manual step; for the other three, look for an IOMMU DMA Protection option in your BIOS and read the release notes, because a firmware update may not enable it for you. That keeps AC 4 satisfied (all three facts stated) without asserting three vendor instructions that no source supports.

## 10. MSI and ASRock status: report what CERT/CC records, claim nothing further

CERT/CC's note (last revised 22 December 2025) records **"No statement received"** from both MSI and ASRock. Secondary press (CyberInsider) reports MSI's Intel 600/700 patches complete and ASRock's shipped for all but 500-series. I could verify neither vendor page.

Decision: the hub page says CERT/CC recorded no vendor statement from MSI or ASRock, gives the date of that record, and says we could not confirm their current status. We do not claim a fix exists, and we do not claim one doesn't. This satisfies AC 3.

## 11. Dates: say "December 2025" in prose, exact dates in the sources block

The sources disagree by a day — CERT/CC published 2025-12-17; NVD gives 12/16/2025 for CVE-2025-14302/14303/14304 and 12/17/2025 for CVE-2025-11901. Rather than assert one day, prose says "December 2025" and the sources block carries each exact date next to its own source. CLAUDE.md rule 5, applied to someone else's dates.

# CAUTIONS

1. **No vendor page was verifiable from here, and AC 1 cannot be discharged by an agent.** GIGABYTE and MSI return 403, ASRock returns an empty body, ASUS renders its advisory table client-side. These are almost certainly fine in a browser — 403 to an automated fetcher is bot protection, not a dead link — but nobody has confirmed any of them lists this advisory or what models it names. The copy is written so that does not matter: it links them as "security advisories" and claims nothing about their contents. **Phase 2 is deliberately blocked on a human opening all four.** Same failure mode as the ROG-forum citation on `guide-no-post.html`.

2. **Ordering hazard with #20 / #22.** Both of those add an advisory box to AMD review pages at the *same anchor* (`<h2 id="specifications">`). Seven pages are in both scopes — the GIGABYTE AMD boards: `a620m-ds3h`, `b650-aorus-elite-ax`, `b650i-aorus-ultra`, `b650m-aorus-elite-ax`, `x670e-aorus-master`, `x870-aorus-elite-wifi7`, `x870e-aorus-master`. If both ship, those pages get two stacked security boxes above the spec table. **Recommendation: land #20/#22 first, then Phase 2 of this issue, and at that point merge the two boxes into a single "Security advisories for this board" block with two paragraphs.** That is a design decision neither issue specifies, so it wants a human. Do not let two agents edit the same anchor line on the same page in parallel branches.

3. **Decision 9 corrects the issue body.** The issue generalises the "Enable with Full Protection" instruction to all four vendors' advisories. CERT/CC attributes it to ASUS alone, and the CNA advisories for the other three say only "update firmware." If a vendor page turns out to document the same manual step for GIGABYTE, MSI or ASRock, the copy should be widened — but not before.

4. **Decision 1 changes the page list the issue implies.** The issue's ASUS list would have excluded our two ASUS B760 pages and included chipsets we do not own. The CVE record is the better source and I used it. If the vendor pages disagree with the CVE records at chipset level, that is a genuine conflict worth flagging in the PR rather than resolving silently.

5. **Decision 8 is the cheap one to overrule.** Unlike #20, the CVSS scores here are verified — 7.0 under CVSS 4.0, 6.8 under CVSS 3.1, both CNA-assigned. I omitted them because they crowd out the actionable part, not because they are doubtful. One word puts them back.

6. **Both PRs are `needs-human`.** Phase 1 is 5 files but creates a new page type and a new `guides.html` category. Phase 2 is 32 files, well over the tripwire.

7. **`npm test` is currently dirty from other branches.** Untracked `scripts/tmp-*.test.mjs` files in the working tree fail for unrelated reasons. Task 4 step 3 says how to tell the difference. Do not "fix" them here.

8. **`uefi-dma-security-advisory.html` is a permanent URL.** There is no redirect layer in this repo and CLAUDE.md forbids deleting pages, so the slug cannot be changed after launch. If you want a different name, say so now.
